**Friday 10:00–12:00 CEST · live on Zoom · mandatory.**

You present the **single-cell navigator you built in Wednesday's lab** and the **call you made** on
your data owner's cluster — and the room questions it. Same format as every seminar; this page tunes
it to Week 5. A few of you are drawn at random to present (7 min + 3 min discussion); everyone should
come ready.

## What to show (≈7 minutes, driven from your live app)

- **The question**, in your owner's words — the one decision they had to make.
- **Your navigator, live.** Don't screenshot a slide deck — open the app and *drive* it: colour the
  map by the genes and quality numbers that mattered, land on the cluster in question.
- **The call, with the evidence on screen.** What the cluster actually is, and the genes/QC that say
  so — not what the owner hoped. If you changed their mind, show the moment the data did it.
- **One honest limit** — what you did *not* check, or what would change your answer.

Prefer showing the live app to reading bullet points. If your network is shaky, have a couple of
screenshots as backup, but the app is the star.

## What the room (and we) will push on

- Did you answer the owner's *actual* decision, or just describe the clusters?
- Is your call backed by **marker genes and quality numbers**, or by where cells sit on the map?
- Did you take the owner's instinct on faith, or did the navigator settle it?
- Could a stranger open your **GitHub repo**, read the README, and run your navigator?

## Build your own style

Make the navigator and the README *yours* — a tool and a repo you'd be proud to hand a collaborator.
If you've seen an app or a README you admire, show it to Pi and ask it to take inspiration (say so).
The goal isn't a template everyone copies; it's your own clear, honest, runnable piece of work.
