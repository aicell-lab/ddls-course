Why we're doing this

We train the forward-deployed scientist

  • You take someone else's biological problem.
  • You interview them until the question is precise.
  • You direct an AI agent to do the labour — while you supply the judgement.
Specify→ Direct→ Verify

The scarce skill is specification, direction and verification — the harness, not the model.

Recap

Two agents — and you are the only channel

Nothing passes from A to B except what you write down. That channel is the examined skill.

Lab 1

The goal was to run the whole loop once

  • Interview Agent A → use the portal → set up Pi locally → direct it → produce something, end to end.
  • One full pass, hands on every part of the machinery.
  • The aim was familiarity with the pipeline — not the result at the end of it.
Lab 1

What it was not

  • Not a deep, polished, publication-grade analysis.
  • Not finishing with time to spare.
  • Not completing every validation step.
Remember
A rough, rushed, incomplete-feeling first pass is completely fine — and expected. Week-1 quality is not graded.

Part 1 · the computer lab

How you rated it

A deliberately-stretchy first lab landing near 3.6 overall is a healthy, honest signal — not a disappointing one.

Reading your submissions

What you did remarkably well

  • You refused vague words — you made Agent A define "trustworthy", "unusable", "scaffold class".
  • You asked the killer question — "what would make you NOT trust this result?" — and turned it into must-nots.
  • You read the brief back to check your own understanding.
  • You caught data traps: header mismatches, leakage, temporal splits, "blanks are never zero".
  • You directed Pi rather than trusting it — pushing back, auditing its own spec.

Feedback · 1 of 3

"Setup ate the clock"

  • By far the most common note — Pi, the terminal, permissions, Windows in particular.
  • You're right, and it's a one-time cost: Pi and the portal are set up now. Next week you walk in already installed.
  • We're adding an OS-by-OS setup checklist so the lab window goes to thinking, not installing.

The skill we grade was never "can you install a CLI tool."

Feedback · 2 of 3

"Not enough time to check carefully"

  • The tension between the clock and doing verification properly is real.
  • But Week-1 quality is not graded — rushing the last validation step this week is expected, not a failure.
  • As setup time disappears, more of the window goes to the actual loop.
The skill
Noticing "I didn't have time to check this" and writing it down is itself the job. Many of you did exactly that.
Feedback · 3 of 3

"I wasn't sure what to even ask"

  • Sitting with someone else's problem and not yet knowing the right question — that confusion is the job.
  • It is the hardest part, and it is the point. It gets easier fast.
  • We're tightening the lab's opening so the first move is clearer — without removing the productive struggle.
You asked: release it a week early?

Why the lab comes out the same week

  • Each week's lab is shaped by the previous week's feedback — when we release is itself a data-driven decision. We'd rather not release it blindly.
  • Some weeks bring a live guest lecture — handing out the lab before the topic is introduced wouldn't make sense.
  • The lab runs on the live portal — we keep it healthy in the fixed lab window, not around the clock for a week.
And yes — it's more intense

A bit of stress, on purpose

Dedicated, focused time — plus a little pressure — is how this skill actually sticks. Comfortable and diffuse doesn't build it.

But we care that it's manageable: you may miss one mandatory session — email ddls-course@scilifelab.se before it. If it tips into overwhelming, tell us — that's data too.

Part 2 · the seminar

A weekly checkpoint, not an exam

  • First, we review the computer lab together — the shared debrief we just did.
  • What worked, what tripped people up, what to carry into next week.
  • Then we move on to a few selected presentations.
Part 2 · the seminar

How presentations work

Who
~7–10, at random
Drawn each week. Everyone should be ready — it could be you.
What
Your own work
The work you did in that week's computer lab — not a paper.
How long
7 + 3 minutes
Seven to present, three to discuss. This replaced the old journal club.
The core idea

"You can outsource your thinking, but not your understanding."

Why we present orally

Outsource the doing, not the knowing

  • We made it easy to produce — the AI writes the report, even the slides.
  • That does not make it any easier to understand what was done.
  • To present it out loud, you have to actually understand it — so preparation starts from your own work, not the slides the AI made.
Before the seminar

Be ready to explain your own work

  • Your question — what you were really asked.
  • Your data — and its traps.
  • What the analysis actually did.
  • What you would and would not trust.
To pass the seminar
You must be able to present your work if you are selected.
